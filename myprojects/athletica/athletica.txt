## One-liner

A full-stack gym management platform for an outdoor group-fitness business in
Adelaide, Australia — member bookings, attendance, coaching, nutrition, e-commerce,
subscription billing, and a public marketing site, in a single application.

## Short description (~50 words)

Athletica Roll Call runs an outdoor fitness business end to end. Members book
sessions, follow meal plans and track a six-week challenge; instructors take roll
call and build workouts; admins manage memberships, staff, shop orders and Stripe
subscriptions. It also serves the company's public marketing site from the same
codebase and database.

## Longer description (~150 words)

Athletica is an outdoor group-fitness company running bootcamp-style sessions
across multiple locations. They were coordinating members, attendance, rosters and
payments across spreadsheets and messaging apps.

I built a single platform to replace all of it. Members sign in to book sessions
against their membership entitlements, follow assigned meal plans, log measurements
and photos through a structured six-week challenge, and buy merchandise. Instructors
run live roll call, build sessions from an exercise library, and track attendance.
Admins manage memberships and pricing, staff and rosters, shop inventory and orders,
and a tiered permission system that controls who can see what.

Payments run on Stripe — recurring memberships, prepaid terms, session packs and shop
orders — with a webhook-driven model where entitlements are only ever granted after
Stripe confirms the money settled.

The public marketing site is served from the same application, pulling coaches,
locations, session times and live pricing straight from the admin database.

## Key features

> Pick whichever subset fits the layout. Ordered roughly by how well they
> demo in a screenshot.

**Session booking & roll call**
Members book against their membership entitlement; instructors run live roll call and
mark attendance. Capacity, session times and locations are all admin-managed.

**Membership & billing**
Three membership tiers across monthly, 6-month and 12-month terms, with weekly,
fortnightly and 4-weekly billing options. Session packs and casual sessions sit
alongside subscriptions. All handled through Stripe.

**Six Week Challenge**
A structured transformation program — measurements, progress photos, check-ins,
milestones and assigned meal plans, with a coach-facing view of each participant.

**Nutrition system**
An ingredient database with per-unit macro tracking (per 100 g, per biscuit, per egg),
a recipe builder that calculates macros automatically, meal plan assembly, and
generated shopping lists. Includes bulk CSV import for ingredients and recipes.

**Workout builder**
An exercise library with media, used to compose sessions and assign them to programs.

**Shop**
Product catalogue, cart, discount codes, Stripe checkout and order management.

**Community**
Groups, messaging, announcements, events and an activity-points system.

**Tiered admin permissions**
Six role levels from member through to critical admin, enforced in the database with
Row Level Security rather than only in the interface. Includes admin impersonation for
support, and two-factor authentication.

**Public marketing site**
An animated, responsive marketing site — home, memberships, locations, about, FAQ,
contact and legal pages — served from the same app. Coaches, locations, session times
and pricing come live from the database, so the site can't drift out of date.

## Engineering highlights

> This section is the one worth keeping if space is tight — it's the part that
> shows judgment rather than just feature count.

**Payments where the browser has no authority.**
Nothing in the frontend can grant a membership or add session credits. The browser
sends only a plan code, never an amount — prices are always looked up server-side, so a
tampered request can't buy a 20-pack for a dollar. Entitlements are written by a single
Stripe webhook, only after Stripe confirms the payment settled. Every webhook event ID
is recorded before any work happens, so Stripe's at-least-once delivery can't hand out
the same session pack twice.

**Security enforced in the database, not the UI.**
Access rules live in PostgreSQL Row Level Security policies, so they hold regardless of
what the client sends. Anonymous visitors read purpose-built views that expose only
public columns, keeping internal notes out of reach of the marketing site.

**One codebase, two audiences.**
The marketing site and the application share a database but not a bundle — the public
site is code-split, so someone reading the pricing page never downloads the admin
application.

**Accessibility built into the motion work.**
Every animation on the public site collapses to its finished state when a visitor has
asked for reduced motion, including background video, which falls back to a still.
